4. **Root causes of low scoring.** Premature transition handoff removed usable early lanes; ordinary lateral contact was judged as charging; a 900ms selection-to-release chain waited after takeoff before starting another throw preparation. Keeper timing/reach also overprotected penalties and ordinary difficult throws. The same eleven pilot recipes progressed from 34.27 to 45.82 goals. The final paired fifty progress from 34.08 to 47.24; this is a seeded cohort effect rather than an additive per-cause goal decomposition.

5. **Root causes of excessive save percentage.** Penalties received an unconditional full hand/dive radius despite already simulated body movement. Released direction could be read without the keeper reaction delay. Preparation, commitment and release timing also erased opportunity or made a prepared stance inconsistent. Context-specific physical corrections replace those errors. Ordinary pivot/wing baseline conversion was already healthy, so no blanket keeper nerf was justified. The isolated penalty-reach overcorrection (all penalties scoring) is retained in the stages; final penalties are 231/313 goals.

6. **Root causes of weak transition output.** Settled handoff could use a named airborne pass receiver instead of an actual controller and a formed central line could hide an uncontained wide threat. Actual control, playable outlets and goalward containment now govern handoff; a committed finish retains its transition context. Final 263 transition releases advance a mean 25.26m in the live phase and face1.84 goal-side field defenders. Starts, source and aborts remain distinct.

7. **Root causes of high raw foul count.** The retained 19–19 case had 441 physical contacts, 315 legal contacts, 126 assessed fouls, 48 pending/45 realized/3 recalled advantages and 78 inferred stops. Of its fouls, 96 were frontal momentum judgments, 27 behind contacts and three holds; only two repeated the same pair within two seconds. Total speed was mistaken for charging direction, and the balance allowance decreased with better balance. Sparse archived movement supported this diagnosis but was explicitly approximate, not exact committed velocity telemetry.
