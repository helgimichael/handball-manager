# Handball Manager — Management Experience v1

The management game now connects information, investigation, decisions and their saved consequences. Match simulation was kept outside this change. The final production application runs at **http://localhost:3004**.

1. **Product direction.** A desktop club-management workspace with graphite surfaces, a restrained teal accent, native typography and tabular numbers. Compact information and object links take priority over decorative cards.
2. **Navigation architecture.** Persistent groups for the office, team, match/calendar, recruitment and club/world. Club, game date and season stay visible; Save, Continue, back/forward navigation and search remain available. Contextual profiles and matches retain a primary navigation owner.
3. **Home.** Managerkontoret brings together contract risks, availability, live energy, shortlist/report counts, next opponent, recent results, league position/form, transfer capacity, upcoming training and background results. Each summary leads into the relevant decision workspace.
4. **Inbox.** Existing match, medical, training, scouting and competition events remain. Actual contract risks and agreed/completed transfers now generate stable, duplicate-safe messages. Read/archive state persists; renewing a player resolves the old warning. Remaining contract risks survive season rollover. Contract and transfer filters were added.
5. **Player profile.** Contextual recruitment/squad actions, current career ratings, live energy for own players, separate performance/history view, recent match contributions, archived minutes, shooting/save samples and recorded season totals. External players retain knowledge gating. Individual development is shown for own players.
6. **Squad workspace.** Search, primary/secondary-position filtering, availability/energy filters, sorting, editable persistent squad roles and two-player selection. Overview, performance, physical and contract presets expose starts, shooting samples, save samples, actual energy/load, return dates, wages and expiry. Position-depth counts link back into the filters.
7. **Comparison.** Any own or external players can be compared. Public profile, wages, contract, appearances, starts and shooting/goalkeeper samples sit alongside knowledge-aware assessments. Own players have position-relevant numeric attributes; external attributes stay behind scouting's existing visibility rules. Raw CA/PA is absent.
8. **Scouting.** The existing assignment/report system now connects directly from profiles and recruitment into comparison and offers. Calendar deadlines and Home/inbox report links expose progress. A club with no scout must recruit one through the existing staff system.
9. **Player search.** Career-world players can be filtered by name/club, position, maximum age, nationality, competition, expiring contract and free agency. Name, age, knowledge, expiry and goals are sortable; results are paged at 60 rows.
10. **Shortlist.** Persistent recruitment targets, priority and notes are reused. Direct profile/search actions add/remove targets, and Home opens the shortlist view. Scouting and transfer status derive from actual assignments, reports and offers.
11. **Transfers/recruitment.** Profile links open the selected target's offer form. Cash, transfer capacity and monthly wage room are visible. The ledger now reads persistent career offers, including incoming/outgoing direction, player, clubs, date, fee and outcome. Rejected bids can be revised; signing changes the real roster, contract and finances.
12. **Contracts.** A dedicated expiry-ordered squad overview shows dates, days remaining, wages and status, with an urgency filter and contextual renewal pane. Renewals use the existing affordability checks. Agreed wages are now protected from legacy salary recalibration on reload; old completed transfer agreements and active loans also retain their contractual wages.
13. **Training.** Existing weekly templates, session edits, coaches, focus and individual load controls remain functional. The workspace explains calendar execution and connects to player profiles/calendar. A recorded-outcome panel exposes participation and before/after energy from actual executed sessions.
14. **Club pages.** Own and external clubs connect to their competition, current results/form, completed transfers and recorded season finishes. Own-club staff counts use the career roster. Existing own-club finances and partnerships remain accessible.
15. **Competitions.** Canonical competition URLs provide table/form, upcoming fixtures, results, player statistics, clubs and historical seasons. Other competitions expose their clubs honestly without a fabricated table or results.
16. **Statistics.** Goals, appearances, shooting efficiency, saves, save percentage and rating rankings use stored career statistics. Percentages include attempts; efficiency rankings have a minimum-sample control. Team scoring/defence tables include matches and per-match context.
17. **Fixtures/calendar.** Schedule remains the full own-team fixture ledger. Any fixture has a contextual information page rather than misleadingly opening the next match. The four-week calendar joins matches, training, contract/scout dates, scheduled arrivals, transfer-window boundaries and league milestones.
18. **Match context.** Preview adds table/form context and squad/training links. Reports add manager follow-up, current league context, next preparation actions, archived player links and expandable recorded goal/7m/suspension events. Replay still reads the played archive; no report generates a new simulation.
19. **History.** Existing final tables, player season totals, completed transfers and immutable played match archives have a dedicated workspace. Profiles and clubs link into it. Historical statistics explicitly identify the player's current club, since historical roster snapshots are not stored.
20. **Search/navigation.** Global search finds career players, clubs and competitions and opens their canonical objects. Player and club names link across tables, profiles, transfers and results. Competition inbox destinations now address the actual competition.
21. **Design system.** Shared page headings, panels, empty states, fixture tables, object actions, filters, tabs, comparison tables, feed rows and summary strips. Shared sort headers now use keyboard-operable buttons. Focus rings, semantic labels, status text and basic responsive overflow are retained.
22. **Persistence.** Reuses the existing career/save representation for inbox, shortlist, scouting, transfers, contracts, training and history. `PlayerContract.wageAgreed` is an optional backward-compatible field. No destructive save migration or parallel management store was introduced. Filters are ordinary session controls, not advertised as saved views.
23. **Removed weak surfaces.** Placeholder facilities and club-history panels were removed. The CPU debug link was removed from normal club navigation. Disabled title-screen settings/exit entries were removed. Replays are contextual under Schedule rather than a separate primary section.
24. **Deeper workflows.** Expiring contract → profile/performance → comparison → renewal/replacement; positional need → filtered market → shortlist → hired scout → assignment/report → comparison → revised offer → signing → budget/roster; upcoming opponent → club/form → squad/tactics → played match → report/table/player history.
25. **Browser journeys.** Repository Playwright tests cover the seven requested journeys through normal controls, including real scout hiring, calendar advancement to report completion, rejected/revised offers, signing, budget/roster consequences and save/load. Match UI tests exercise lineup edits, substitutions, live tactics, live save/reload, finalization and replay. Competition results also open background-match reports and return to table/statistics/player history. The in-app browser connector was unavailable; screenshots were inspected from the local Playwright artifacts.

Page-local maps/memoized derivations join existing career data; reconciliation skips active simulation advances and avoids rebuilding the world index when no new transfer message is needed. 33. **Meaningful limitations.** Only the manager's league has simulated fixtures/tables; other leagues participate in the existing recruitment world. Historical tables/player totals are recorded, but historical roster/contract snapshots and prior background-match seasons are not. Transfer-window rules remain the existing generic model. Negotiations retain the existing deterministic decisions; there is no new agent or outgoing transfer-list system. Some older analysis copy remains English. Saves remain local to the browser origin, so the isolated QA port has its own careers.

39. **HOW TO QA MANAGEMENT EXPERIENCE.**

    - Create a Tumba career starting eight weeks before league kickoff. Open Home → Inbox → a contract warning → player → performance.
    - Filter/sort the squad, select two players and compare. Open Contracts, renew an expiring player, press Save, reload and confirm the new expiry and resolved warning.
    - Search Division 1 Norra for a target, shortlist and scout. If needed, recruit a free-agent scout through Personal. Continue until a real report is filed; compare and approach the target. Read rejection reasons, revise the offer and inspect cash, wage room and the updated squad.
    - Inspect training, recorded session outcomes, calendar deadlines, another club, competition results and player rankings. Play a match through Preview, then inspect the report, current table, player performance and saved replay.
    - Save/reload at the same URL. Check the shortlist, inbox state, negotiated contract and transfer ledger. For the longer proof, run `npm run test:season:management --workspace=@handball-manager/game`.
