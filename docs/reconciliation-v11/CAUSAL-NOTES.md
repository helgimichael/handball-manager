# Narrow causal changes

The starting engine's field conversion was already close to the HBL environment. Its attack lifecycle lost opportunities after containment: an old carrier relationship could survive a physical return; a contained position could keep the attack in finishing mode; an established entry could survive its own good recycle; a named return receiver could continue an obsolete run; and a prepared stationary receiver fell back to the original static selector. None of those defects requires a weaker shot window or a goal bonus.

The repair clears the old carrier relationship on actual acquisition while retaining possession, velocity and the current defensive state. A close carrier enters finishing mode only with the existing valid opportunity and acceptable containment. A good recycle retires an established entry, then permits the existing shape coordinator to establish the next one. A named back checks the actual return using the normal receiving executor. Time spent preparing that matching physical receipt can satisfy part of the existing court-read budget; ordinary receipts retain their original budget. That prepared receiver reads the existing Intelligence affordances. A contained first threat may physically withdraw and reconnect, and a safe trailing back can be valued for its next launching position. No second threat or shot is guaranteed; passive-play and handling authority remain intact.

Defensive geometry, marks, help, handoff, recovery and global advantage are retained. The changes use the defence's actual remaining displacement rather than inventing a new secondary-advantage system. A carrier with a clear uncontested route continues the original Intelligence path. Created bind/pass receivers retain their existing priority.

The discipline defect was an overly binary shoulder threshold: a defender could lose the near shoulder and illegally restrain progression before crossing the full duel-beaten margin. A separate contact read recognizes that partial loss, without declaring a defender beaten or changing defensive allocation. Hold selection grades actual lost position and discipline. Near-trunk rear bent-arm restraint of an executing, moving penetration can enter the existing punishable category. Light or stationary rear contact and legal side containment do not inherit that severity. Ordinary fouls do not escalate from a global count. The existing progressive warning/sanction contract remains authoritative. Clear-chance destruction and discipline are independent; penalty-award geometry is unchanged.

Penalty resolution previously compressed execution uncertainty and did not distinguish finishing/composure sufficiently. It also gave a loaded penalty keeper fully reaction-free hand reach. Penalty placement now includes finishing and composure, and execution spread remains capability-dependent. A penalty keeper moves normally to a bounded legal depth, loads a penalty-specific finite step, and retains most anticipation credit while paying a small residual hand read cost. The ordinary field placement expression, spread multiplier, loading step and reaction branch are unchanged. Outcomes still come from seeded throwing execution and swept keeper/ball interaction. Shooter and keeper capability remain distinct; no conversion target or miss quota is read.

The added cycle observer is developer-only, consumes canonical events and ledger records, and writes neither state nor RNG. Its threat and second-attack counts are motor/episode proxies, not counts of new possessions. Exclusive possession endings distinguish attacks with any shot from non-shot loss classes and procedural endings. Free throws, recycles and dead-ball restarts are non-exclusive continuation events. Historical result turnovers also include attacks with earlier shots, so those totals must not be described as exact non-shot endings.

The referee remains an implementation of the game's existing rule contract, not a claim of complete IHF rule coverage. Body-directed progressive punishment and clear-chance awards follow the distinctions in the [official IHF indoor rules](https://www.ihf.info/sites/default/files/2025-07/09A%20-%20Rules%20of%20the%20Game_Indoor%20Handball_E.pdf). No new every-third-foul policy or disqualification framework was added.
