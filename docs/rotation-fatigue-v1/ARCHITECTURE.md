# Energy and functional fatigue

Human QA: Torslanda 22–33 IFK Tumba. Tumba used zero substitutions; several starters played 60 minutes with reported energies 42–57%. A 22–8 halftime lead became an 11–14 second half. This establishes numerical fatigue and an observed half split, but does not isolate fatigue from score, tactics, opportunity mix or chance. It does not prove rotation should reverse that result.

Before this task, conditionAfter charged official on-court time and squared travel speed, scaled by ENDURANCE. Non-active states recovered .000025 energy per 50ms, including simulated halftime (up to 30 percentage points over ten minutes). Career matches instead compressed halftime to zero ticks and did not execute this recovery. Movement and spatial arrival shared .76+.24*energy for both speed and acceleration. At 30% energy a player still had 83.2% of both. Execution capacity bottomed at 80%; placement, passing, catches, keeper hands and blocks read it. Shot context, pivot movement, entry-family selection and control-read timing also read energy. Intelligence added only a small fatigue risk and kept free-lane priorities. No recent-work memory existed. Fatigue was functional, but first step and repeated recovery had the same modest scale as cruising, and long breaks/bench recovery used one rate.

After this task, cruising retains the previous movement scale. Court acceleration applies a smooth energy-loss-squared term and current acute exertion; the physical integrator and secondsTo use the same acceleration. No target, geometry, duty ownership, beaten flag or defender identity is rewritten by fatigue. Helping, recovering, retreating, changing direction and attacking again inherit their actual physical arrival limits. Contact/sanction mechanics retain their causal reads; slower recovery may create a real lost shoulder, without a fatigue foul lottery.

Acute exertion is one optional 0–1 canonical player-condition value. It follows squared normalized speed plus executing/committed drive, dribble, jump, shot, screen, contact and block load, with endurance-sensitive accumulation and decay. Real actions, rather than positional or tactic multipliers, create differing workloads. Quiet phases, bench and halftime clear recent load. Energy retains speed/endurance cost with a small demanding-action cost. Bench/halftime recover at a reduced gradual rate; timeout court recovery is small. Energy stays bounded .15–1 for court players, .3–1 for keepers. These are explicit simulation choices, not a physiology claim.

Difficulty-sensitive execution adds a bounded loss to existing capacity only for the actual throw/catch problem. Ordinary shots read pressure, range and body speed; penalties retain the v1.1 resolution expression. Passes read the existing range, pressure, receiver movement, lane and urgency difficulty. Catches read existing pressure/alignment/pace/movement difficulty. A trivial action gets no additional loss. No extra RNG draw or fixed miss is introduced. Existing shot selection and opportunity geometry remain authoritative.

Keeper fatigue/anticipation/reach and energy rates remain on their prior branches. No keeper rotation requirement is introduced. Existing AI rotation policy is preserved; no human-side automatic policy or mandatory threshold is added. Incoming players retain their own energy/exertion, identity and accumulated minutes; stale own perception/entry memory is cleared when staging their real exchange. No energy is copied from the outgoing actor. Archives retain existing minutes/energy load fields; active JSON retains acute memory. No archive DTO rewrite or UI change.
